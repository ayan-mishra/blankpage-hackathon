You have forty sleep recordings. Which ones can you trust?

People wear a small headband that records their brain activity while they sleep at home. Sometimes the headband slips, loses contact with the skin, or stops recording for part of the night.

One 2026 study checked 100 nights recorded this way. 74 were fine, 16 were partly bad and 10 failed completely. That’s about one in four nights you can’t fully trust.

Without a check, you only find out after you’ve analyzed the data, or you never do. Source: medRxiv preprint, not yet peer reviewed

Drop in the file. It’s read right here, in your browser.

Somnium scans every channel in five-second windows and marks what looks wrong. Nothing is uploaded.

Every mark comes with its reason.

Click a mark to see the measurement, the rule that tripped, and what usually causes it. You can check the reasoning instead of trusting a score.

Your lab’s standard, written your way.

Type a rule in plain words. Or drag across a stretch you know is bad and let Somnium propose the threshold. Or tune the numbers yourself. Each rule shows what it would flag before you add it.
